Why Harsyntra

Manpower solutions, built differently from typical scheduling tools

Most tools were made for a single shop and grew locations, permissions, and labor reporting as afterthoughts. Harsyntra was designed from the start for whole organizations — here is the honest side-by-side.

HarsyntraTypical scheduling tools
Manpower-only mode Run scheduling and hours with every money feature hidden — no rates, no costs, just manpower, until you choose to turn labor costs on. Money and cost features are bundled in from day one, whether your organization wants them or not.
Pricing Quoted per organization, based on your size, locations, and the features you use. One published per-seat price that every organization has to fit into.
Role permissions Granular permissions per role, a reporting hierarchy, and an Owner who has full control of their own organization — and only their own. A few fixed roles — admin, manager, employee — with little in between.
Labor cost analysis Scheduled hours sit next to the hours actually worked, with variance and the cost of the week visible before you publish — not after. Hours get exported to a spreadsheet to work out what the week actually cost.
Weekly overtime rules Overtime is calculated on the week — over 40 hours — the way hourly teams actually run, and flagged while you schedule. Daily overtime rules only, or no overtime tracking at all.
Labor-cost preview See exactly what a week costs before you publish it — like the demo in the hero above — with warnings for overtime and days with no manager on duty. The cost of a schedule only becomes clear after the fact, once the week is already worked.
Audit log Changes are recorded — who did what, and when — across the organization. No reliable record of who changed a shift, a time entry, or a pay rate.
Multi-location support Multi-company and multi-location by design — one platform for the whole organization, with data strictly separated between organizations. Built for a single shop; extra locations are bolted on or need separate accounts.

Compared against the typical feature set of general-purpose scheduling tools — not any single product.
